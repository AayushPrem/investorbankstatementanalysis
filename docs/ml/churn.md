# Model card: customer churn (Phase 6)

> **It recovers our own simulator.** Every number here comes from `python -m ml.churn.train` / `evaluate`, run on 2026-11-02 on **synthetic corpus v1** (`corpus_hash 8a28bbe2…f3d1`, `splits_hash a905e9f1…9efe`, generator 2.0.0). Churn in that corpus is generated by the latent-health model in `docs/ml/corpus_card.md`. A good score means the model recovered that simulator, not real customer behaviour.

| | |
|---|---|
| Model version | `churn1-hgb_l15-8a28bbe2-2023be` (artifact gitignored; `metadata.json` committed) |
| Algorithm | scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingClassifier` (15 leaves, lr 0.05, 400 iterations, `class_weight="balanced"`), plus a Platt calibrator fitted on out-of-fold train scores |
| Category input | `cat3-svc_cal-company-8a28bbe2-191ad9` predictions, LLM off. `true_category` is never read. |
| Customer identity | Narration counterparty key (`counterparty_from_narration`), see [Identity](#identity) |
| Split | `splits/churn_temporal.parquet`: temporal and company-disjoint |
| Serving | `CustomerAnalyticsAnalyst.churn_risk` (`AGENT_VERSIONS`: `customer_analytics` 1.1.0, `ml_churn` 1.0.0) |

## Label (verbatim)

**Customer makes no payment in (cutoff, cutoff+90d] given ≥2 payments before cutoff.**

- Unit: (company, customer key, cutoff). Cutoffs are the month ends in `churn_temporal.parquet`.
- "Payment" means a predicted-REVENUE credit whose narration key isn't an aggregator settlement.
- The label is computed from the transactions themselves (`ml.features.churn_features.label_at`). **No latent label is used for the target, for fitting, or for any choice.**
- *Active* unit: last payment within 90 days before the cutoff. Serving scores active customers only, so active units are the **primary** population. All units are reported too: 22.0% of train units and 29.2% of test units have already been silent for more than 90 days (from the split table below), and that is where the rule fires.

## Identity

The pipeline's `CustomerIdentityResolver` (embeddings off, as the pipeline runs it) leaves UTR/reference tokens in the name on corpus narrations. 95% of its ids have a single row, so it can't form units that need ≥ 2 payments. The resolver can't change in this phase without moving gold `customer_id_accuracy` (0.6662) and every rule churn event.

So units use the Phase 5 narration key, computed identically at train and serve time. At serving, each active resolver `customer_id` takes the risk of the key that covers most of its rows.

The audit below is from `evaluate.py` (evaluation only; it reads `labels/…true_customer_id`). It covers predicted-REVENUE, non-aggregator credits of the three churn profiles:

| identity | purity | completeness | ids / company (median) | true / company | true customers split | ids with one row |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| resolver (pipeline) | 0.997 | **0.195** | 150 | 19 | 0.908 | 0.949 |
| narration key (used) | 0.998 | **0.871** | 31 | 19 | 0.566 | 0.278 |

**Predicted REVENUE**: every predicted-REVENUE row in these profiles is true REVENUE (1.0).

**Cost of the key's splits** (test, evaluation only):
- Key-built labels agree with true-id-built labels on **0.840** of 5,783 units.
- Units built on `true_customer_id` (an oracle the pipeline never has) have an active base rate of **0.075**, against **0.186** with the key. Some key-level "churn" is a minor narration variant of a customer going quiet.
- Scored by the same frozen model, the oracle units get PR-AUC 0.503 and ROC-AUC 0.852.
- So part of what the model learns is "this key is a sporadic variant", not customer churn.

## Features (`ml/features/churn_features.py`, FEATURE_VERSION 1, rows ≤ cutoff only)

`tenure_days`, `n_payments`, `gap_mean`, `gap_cv` (inter-payment gaps), `days_since_last`, `recency_ratio` (days since last ÷ median gap), `amount_slope_last3` (slope of log amount over the last 3 payments), `amount_last_vs_mean`, `revenue_share` (key's share of company revenue, trailing 365 days), `late_ratio` (share of the last 6 gaps > 1.25 × median gap), `payments_90d`, `company_active_keys`, `company_rev_trend` (log of revenue in the last 90 days ÷ the 90 days before).

- **Change from §12.1.** "Profile one-hot" was replaced by the two observable company-context features. `profile` is a label table column that serving never has.
- **No look-ahead.** `features_at` slices `date <= cutoff` before computing anything. `tests/test_churn_features.py` shows that changing, deleting or adding rows after the cutoff changes no feature, and that changing a row before it does. A further test runs the real categoriser on a statement with and without its future rows, and the features are identical.
- **Training never opens `labels/`.** `tests/test_ml_churn.py` guards `pd.read_parquet` for this.

## Selection (all on TRAIN; the validation set is small)

| split | units | active | companies | company-cutoffs with units | base rate (all / active) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| train | 74,200 | 57,882 | 442 | 3,239 | 0.326 / 0.175 |
| val | 1,592 | 1,247 | 41 | 73 | 0.297 / 0.136 |
| test | 5,835 | 4,133 | 34 | 205 | 0.381 / 0.186 |

Validation has 82 company-cutoffs, and 73 of them have units. That is too small to tune on. Instead:

- **Model, calibration, threshold and `min_history`** are chosen from 5-fold `GroupKFold` (by company) out-of-fold predictions on train. The grid is coarse: 4 configs.

  | candidate | OOF PR-AUC (active) | OOF PR-AUC (all) | OOF ROC-AUC (active) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | LR C=0.1 | 0.506 | 0.833 | 0.829 |
  | LR C=1 | 0.506 | 0.834 | 0.829 |
  | HGB depth 3 | 0.534 | 0.851 | 0.840 |
  | **HGB 15 leaves** | **0.535** | 0.851 | 0.838 |

- **Decision threshold:** 0.275 (max OOF F1 = 0.562 on calibrated active units). The medium band is 0.1749 (OOF active prevalence).
- **`min_history_payments` = 2.** The model beats the days-since-last rule in every history bucket (OOF active AP):

  | bucket | model | rule |
  |---|---|---|
  | [2,3) | 0.653 | 0.582 |
  | [3,4) | 0.580 | 0.478 |
  | [4,6) | 0.427 | 0.311 |
  | [6,∞) | 0.282 | 0.220 |

  Customers with fewer than 2 payments fall outside the label population. They get the untuned rule "silent > 60 days → elevated".
- **Pre-registered validation check:** switch to the LR if the paired company-bootstrap 95% CI of (PR-AUC winner − PR-AUC best LR) on val active units lies entirely below 0.
  - Result: +0.035 [−0.008, 0.084], so no switch.
  - Val PR-AUC: HGB 0.560 [0.442, 0.665], LR 0.524 [0.409, 0.640], on 1,247 units from 37 companies.
  - The CIs overlap almost completely. HGB is kept because CV chose it, not because val shows it is better.

## Caveats

- **Synthetic simulator.** 55% of churners "signal", and that signal is deliberately weak (corpus card). Everything above measures recovery of that design.
- **Identity noise.** See [Identity](#identity): label agreement is 0.840, and the oracle base rate is 0.075 against 0.186.
- **Services firms** are hardest (PR-AUC 0.299). Their lumpy billing (skip probability 0.35) makes 90-day silence common for customers who haven't churned.
- **Small evaluation sets.** Test is 34 companies; the CIs above are the honest spread.
- **Statement dates** run past "today" (up to 2026-11-30). Serving uses the statement's last transaction date as the cutoff, not the wall clock.
- The model was trained from an uncommitted working tree on base 34f59a8 (`git_dirty: true` in metadata).
